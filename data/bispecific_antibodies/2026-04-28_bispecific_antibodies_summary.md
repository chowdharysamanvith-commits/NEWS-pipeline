1. Overview
The pharmaceutical landscape for bispecific antibodies is characterized by significant clinical volatility, capital market activity, and regulatory milestones across the biotechnology sector. Compass Therapeutics faced a market correction after its DLL4xVEGF-A bispecific antibody, tovecimig, failed to meet an overall survival endpoint in biliary tract cancer despite progression-free survival improvements. Conversely, Hemab Therapeutics secured approximately $180 million through an initial public offering to fund Phase 3 development of its lead bispecific antibody, sutacimig, for clotting disorders. Regeneron Pharmaceuticals reported regulatory outcomes for bispecific programs, including an approval for myeloma and a rejection for a cancer indication in mid-2025. Manufacturing capabilities expanded as WuXi Biologics obtained South Korean GMP certification for commercial bispecific antibody production. Strategic partnerships emerged, including a $2.56 billion licensing agreement between Earendil Labs and Sanofi to advance bispecific antibodies for autoimmune diseases. Clinical data from Merck & Co. and Zanidatamab trials indicated continued industry interest in the modality for non-small cell lung cancer and gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma. 4. Clinical & Scientific Highlights
• Tovecimig — Mechanism of action: DLL4xVEGF-A bispecific antibody administered with chemotherapy. — Trial phase: Phase 2/3. — Patient population: 168 adults with unresectable advanced, metastatic, or recurrent biliary tract cancer. — Comparator treatment: Chemotherapy alone. — Clinical endpoints: Median progression-free survival 4.7 months versus 2.6 months for control; median overall survival 8.9 months versus 9.4 months for control (https://fiercebiotech.com/biotech/janux-axes-solid-tumor-t-cell-engager-after-poor-phase-1-showing)
• Sutacimig — Mechanism of action: Bispecific antibody designed for clotting disorders. — Trial phase: Phase 2 completed, Phase 3 planned. — Patient population: Patients with Glanzmann thrombasthenia. — Outcome: Clinically meaningful bleeding reduction achieved (https://fiercebiotech.com/biotech/seaport-hemab-chart-courses-180m-ipos-fund-depression-clotting-candidates)
• JANX008 — Mechanism of action: Masked T-cell engager (TCE) designed to bind epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) on cancer cells and CD3 on T cells. — Trial phase: Phase 1. — Outcome: Discontinued due to subpar magnitude and consistency of activity (https://fiercebiotech.com/biotech/janux-axes-solid-tumor-t-cell-engager-after-poor-phase-1-showing)
• MK-2010 — Mechanism of action: Bispecific antibody. — Trial phase: Phase 1/2. — Patient population: PD-L1-positive NSCLC. — Clinical endpoints: 55% ORR in untreated patients; 18% ORR in previously treated patients at 20 mg/kg dose (https://www.labiotech.eu/trends-news/aacr-2026-cancer-therapeutics/)
• Zanidatamab — Mechanism of action: HER2-targeting bispecific mAb. — Trial phase: Phase 3 HERIZON-GEA-01. — Patient population: HER2-positive gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma. — Clinical endpoints: Median PFS 12.4 months (Arm C) vs 8.1 months (Arm A) (https://cancernetwork.com/view/3-things-you-should-know-about-the-evolving-therapeutic-landscape-of-gea)
• BCMA-directed bispecifics — Mechanism of action: T-cell redirection and activation; Anti-CD38 depletes regulatory T cells. — Patient population: Early relapse multiple myeloma. — Safety: Cytokine release syndrome and infections are key adverse events; prophylactic tocilizumab reduces severity to grade 1 (https://cancernetwork.com/view/discussing-proper-utilization-of-bcma-bispecifics-in-early-relapse-multiple-myeloma)
